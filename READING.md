# Reading

For any reader, and especially a model rereading this repository cold.

Read `specs/WHOLE.md` first. Read this before writing anything about it.

This file states no ontology. Where it and `specs/WHOLE.md` disagree,
`specs/WHOLE.md` governs. It lists readings the kernel excludes. Each has been
reached repeatedly in conversation, by people and by models, and each will be
reached again.

## The question

Not *how do levels relate?* but *what counts as one thing?*

A continuity is a criterion of identity. It fixes what counts as one, what
makes it the same one later, and which differences are changes in it rather
than its replacement. Causal and compositional talk comes after: there is
nothing to relate until the relata are fixed.

The default frame — levels, emergence, reduction, downward causation — answers
a later question and presupposes an answer to this one. Every misreading below
is that frame returning.

## Misreadings

Each entry: the sentence that feels right, why it is refused, and where.

### 1. Levels

*Feels right.* Gliders emerge from cells. The glider constrains its cells. Read
as a production or a constraint running from one articulation to the other.

*Refused.* There is one change, and both continuities trace it. Nothing runs
between them alongside it.

Causal sentences across criteria are not thereby malformed. *The collision of
two gliders made these cells change state* can be true: had the gliders not
collided, those cells would not have changed. It is a dependency between
variables fixed by different criteria over the same occurrences. What is refused
is reading it as a second cause, or as a force between articulations.

Composition is different. It does not hold across quotients at all.

So the theory cannot be recruited for downward causation as an added cause, for
formal causes read as forces, or for fields that organize matter. Each needs
something acting between articulations, and there is only the one change.

*Correction.* An earlier version of this entry called every cross-criterion
causal sentence malformed. That was T3 again: a history determines the
formation.

*Where.* WHOLE: one change carries both continuities. FINDINGS: composition
holds only within an articulation, and says nothing about causation; the
determination/constitution slide recurs.

### 2. Substrate and description

*Feels right.* The cells are what is really there. The glider is a
redescription of them.

*Refused.* Cell-sameness is itself a continuity, and its elements form a
quotient: cell-histories. Neither quotient is the occurrences. That the glider
is derivable from the cell rule says how the case was given, not which identity
is real.

*Where.* EMERGENCE: derivability and a different criterion of identity are
compatible facts.

### 3. Instant and whole

*Feels right.* The elemental articulation sees instants. The whole is what
persists through time.

*Refused.* Both are persistences. A cell persists through changes of state at
one site. A glider persists through changes of site. A frame holds neither,
only a footprint. The difference is what each holds fixed, not whether it
extends in time.

*Where.* WHOLE: identity can persist in two cross-cutting ways.

### 4. Observer

*Feels right.* Change what you observe and you change what exists.

*Refused.* Changing articulation changes the inventory, not the occurrences.
And an articulation is not free: it must be carried by the changing, or it is
laid across it. Nobody has to be watching.

*Where.* EMERGENCE: uncarried. WHOLE: open item 2. FINDINGS: a part is a
persistence.

### 5. Determination

*Feels right.* If the pattern has its own identity, it must have its own
dynamics, or constrain the history beyond what initial data fix.

*Refused.* Identity and determination are separate axes. Forward generation,
two-time boundary conditions, global constraint and variational formulations
are all claims about determination. ≡ says the same under each.

*Where.* WHOLE: identity and determination are separate axes. FINDINGS: the
determination/constitution slide recurs.

### 6. A form's state

*Proposed, under review.*

*Feels right.* A glider's state is its configuration.

*Refused.* Configuration is the footprint, read in the cell quotient. What stays
the same glider through its configurations is its identity. Its state is what
varies within that identity: phase, position, heading. Put configuration in the
state and glider-sameness collapses into arrangement-sameness, which nests.

*Where.* FINDINGS: two gliders on one board share an arrangement and are not
the same glider.
