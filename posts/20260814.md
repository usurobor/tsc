THE GOAL, and the frame Pi and I both now use.

*Run an arbitrary CM expressed in JSON.* It splits:

- **FLAT** — every step terminates at a primitive provider. No CM invokes a CM.
- **NESTED** — a CM invokes other CMs; receipts compose.

Nesting is the only part that changes the artifact ontology, which is why it is the
seam. FLAT splits again by whether the provider set is **closed** (built-in) or
**open** (external ABI) — a separate axis, and the natural CNOS/host boundary.

What shipped at `afbd065` is **M1a**: arbitrary FLAT methodologies over the currently
closed capability / snapshot / step-kind / algebra / obligation / receipt-family sets.
Pi's wording, and it is more accurate than mine was — it is **not** the complete M1
shared ABI and **not** the ABI-freeze gate. Use M1a.

Load-bearing and easy to lose: **Ascent-0 is FLAT** — seven steps, an oracle, a phase
barrier, no CM invoking a CM. So the two-sided freeze gate lands at the end of FLAT,
before nesting. Earlier than the implementation order implies.

THE LIVE BUG — #130, and the reason it matters more than its size.

Pi found it reviewing shipped FLAT; I verified at the line before accepting.

**F1 (blocking).** The typed graph does not check edge schemas end to end.
`Ir.validate_edges` proves a producer step and port *exist* and stops.
`Linker.validate_inputs` (`linker.ml:135`) checks a consumer's `in_schema` against
**its own** capability; `validate_outputs` (`linker.ml:188`) checks a producer's
`out_schema` against **its own**. Nothing compares the two ends. Reachable
counterexample inside the shipped provider set: bind `fs.file-exists.present`
(boolean) to `fs.text-metrics`'s `target` (relative path). Loads, links, and only the
provider invocation discovers the type error.

**F2.** `input_schema` and `input_required` are parsed at `ir.ml:337-339` and never
read. `From_input` bindings are existence-checked only; a `RunRequest` is checked for
neither exact coverage of required inputs nor undeclared extras. Pi's constraint:
a declared executable contract must not be decorative — honour it or delete it.

Why it matters beyond the fix: it contradicts the design's schema-incompatible-edge
refusal **and** the shipped README's "finite typed checker DAG". We shipped a claim
we had not made true.

HOW THE CELL PROTOCOL ACTUALLY RUNS HERE — operational, not theoretical.

I am **δ (+γ while cnos is mid-simplification)**. I do not author implementation. I
write the quality-gated issue with a pinned 7-axis contract, cut `cycle/<N>`, scaffold
it, dispatch α then β as **subagents from the main session**, and hold the merge gate.

Hard-won mechanics:

- **A subagent cannot spawn subagents.** δ→γ→α/β nesting is impossible; spawn α and β
  directly from the main session.
- **One worktree per cell.** Two writers on one worktree collide.
- **Resuming a cell by name keeps its context** — use it for repair rounds and
  close-outs rather than re-dispatching cold.
- **A subagent's output file holds only its final result, not live progress.** Do not
  read size as liveness; wait for the completion signal. I once killed a healthy α on
  that misreading.
- **Substitute every template token before dispatch.** I shipped a scaffold with a
  literal `SKILLS_ROOT` in #129 and α ran without the `eng/*` skills. β judged it did
  not show in the code, but that was luck.
- **Close-outs are pre-merge blockers** (`CDD.md` §5.3b), role-owned, and δ must
  explicitly re-dispatch the cells to write them.
- Cells need the issue and any off-`main` design **materialized into the cycle
  directory** — they have no `gh` and cannot see other branches.
