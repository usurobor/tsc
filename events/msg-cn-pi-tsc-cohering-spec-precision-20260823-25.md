# Cohering


**Status:** Draft


This specification answers one question: what does it mean for one cohering to
articulate itself through distinct vantages? It defines the semantic foundation
and the foundational reading of `≡`. The C≡ specification owns the full grammar
and rewrite laws.


## 1. All is cohering


**Axiom.** All is cohering, and cohering is all.


`All` means that nothing exists independently of cohering. `Cohering` names
activity, not a thing or a finished state.


Nothing outside cohering can bound it. Every boundary and whatever establishes
that boundary must itself be an articulation within cohering. Cohering is
therefore **unlimited**: no external limit contains it, and no determinate
articulation exhausts it.


Unlimited does not mean infinitely large, unconstrained, or without internal
limits. Cohering articulates limited forms without being exhausted by them.


## 2. Cohering articulates itself


**Construction rule.** Cohering articulates itself.


To articulate is to bring a distinction into expression. Because nothing lies
outside cohering, articulation is self-limitation: cohering articulates a limit
within itself. It neither divides into independent substances nor joins things
that existed separately.


The articulation is limited and determinate. The cohering that articulates it
remains unlimited.


## 3. One process has three vantages


The construction rule exposes three vantages. Since all is cohering, each is
cohering:


```text
cohering cohering cohering
```


Labeling each vantage by its role gives:


```text
coherer cohering cohered
```


- **Coherer:** cohering from the vantage of what articulates.
- **Cohering:** the generative articulation in act.
- **Cohered:** cohering from the vantage of what is articulated.


TSC also calls these vantages vertical, generative, and horizontal. These are
positions in one occurrence, not permanent properties of particular things.


The coherer and cohered are two distinguishable poles of one cohering. The
cohering is their complete occurrence, not a third pole, midpoint, bridge, or
hidden substance. Unity belongs to the complete occurrence.


This is **one-as-two**: one indivisible cohering articulates as two poles without
making them identical or separate. The two poles express the distinction; the
generative vantage expresses their unity in act.


## 4. Two poles define a dimension


An **articulation dimension** is a particular distinction through which one
cohering articulates itself as two ordered poles.


The pair defines the dimension. Neither pole defines it alone:


```text
coherer | cohered
observer | observed
thinker | thought
```


The poles are distinct within the dimension and inseparable from the cohering
that articulates them. Their order records a chosen orientation. Reversing the
order changes that orientation; equivalence is not assumed.


One-as-two is the smallest explicit contrast, not an exhaustive count of
vantages. The same cohering may articulate as many determinate vantages. Each
selected pole-pair defines a dimension. This is **one-as-many**: one cohering
generates many articulations without ceasing to be one.


## 5. `≡` expresses a dimension


The foundational forms are:


```text
≡                 cohering with no articulation selected
≡ ≡ ≡             three open vantages
A ≡ B             open dimension: poles A and B, generative center open
A G B             candidate frame: G occupies the generative center
```


These forms juxtapose positions; they are not equations. In `A ≡ B`, `A` and
`B` name the ordered poles, while `≡` occupies the generative position and
leaves its specific cohering open. It is not equality, a separator, a directed
arrow from `A` to `B`, or a claim that the poles mean the same thing.


`A ≡ B` is an open frame: `A` and `B` occupy the poles, while `≡` holds the
unresolved generative position. It asks what one cohering articulates as both
poles. A resolution replaces that center with a candidate `G`, producing the
frame `A G B`.


## 6. Resolution is generative


To resolve `A ≡ B` as `A G B` is to identify a generative cohering `G` that:


1. articulates as `A` from one declared vantage and `B` from another;
2. preserves the difference between the poles;
3. explains why both arise together without reducing either to the other;
4. generates a consequence not contained in the pole labels; and
5. states a condition under which the proposed resolution fails.


`G` cannot be a generic category containing both poles, their conjunction, a
blend, or a mediator placed between them. Although it occupies the center
position, it is the complete generative articulation made explicit, not a third
peer.


The axiom guarantees that no pole lies outside cohering. It does not identify
the specific cohering by which two poles arise; otherwise “cohering” would
vacuously resolve every pair.


**Ascent** moves from the poles to a candidate `G` and fills the frame `A G B`.
**Descent** uses `G` to recover the poles and new consequences. A resolution
succeeds only when both moves hold.


## 7. Cohering recurs


**Closure rule.** Every articulated pole remains cohering and may articulate
through the same form. Recurrence counts as further articulation only when it
exposes a new distinction or consequence.


This construction has recurring properties:


- **Co-constitutive:** the poles and their generative cohering arise in one
  occurrence; none has ontological priority.
- **Irreducible:** no pole substitutes for another, and no pole alone expresses
  the dimension.
- **Complete:** a resolved dimension contains both poles and their generative
  articulation. Completeness does not exhaust cohering.
- **Recursive:** any articulated pole may articulate again.
- **Fractal:** the same form can recur at different depths. This is structural
  self-similarity, not a claim about geometric scale or fractal dimension.
- **Holographic:** any pole can unfold through the whole form because it remains
  cohering. It does not contain all the content of every other articulation.
- **Coinductive:** no finite unfolding exhausts cohering; every current end
  remains open to further articulation.


## 8. Example dimensions


```text
1 ≡ 0
Limited ≡ Unlimited
I ≡ AM
```


Under their familiar readings, these expressions select three different
dimensions of cohering: digital distinction, self-limitation, and
self-articulation. Their labels do not carry a universal interpretation, and the
expressions alone do not supply their generative resolutions.


The `Unlimited` pole is relative to the dimension `Limited ≡ Unlimited`. It is
not automatically identical to cohering-as-all, whose unlimitedness was defined
in §1.


## 9. The cone illustrates resolution


Consider:


```text
triangle ≡ circle      open
triangle cone circle   resolved
```


Under specified orthographic vantages, the outline of a finite right circular
cone is an isosceles triangle from a direction perpendicular to its axis and a
circle from the axial direction. The triangle and circle are not pieces of the
cone or versions of each other. They are distinct projections of one generative
shape.


The cone resolves the expression within that construction: ascent identifies a
form that can generate both projections; descent recovers both and predicts
which other projections the same cone can produce. The cone is not a third
mediator between the shapes. It represents their complete generative
articulation.


A triangle and circle do not uniquely prove a cone without projection and shape
constraints. That limit is essential: a proposed resolution must generate
further consequences capable of distinguishing it from alternatives.


The cone therefore illustrates one-as-many: one cohering generates many
determinate articulations without ceasing to be one.
