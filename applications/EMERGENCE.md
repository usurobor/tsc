# Emergence and Reduction

An application of `specs/WHOLE.md`. It carries the instances the theory
deliberately excludes, and cites the theory rather than restating it.

Nothing here is required to understand the theory. The theory is required to
understand this.

## Exhibits

Two cases, used throughout, chosen because in both the two identity relations
demonstrably fail to coincide (A3).

**The wave.** A body of water articulated by parcels, pressures and momentum
transfer; and articulated as crest, trough, propagation, interference. The same
parcel participates in many waves; the same wave persists through continuously
changing parcels. Sameness of parcel is not sameness of wave, in both
directions.

The water does not assemble molecules into a wave. The water waves.

**The glider.** A cellular field articulated by cells and neighbour relations;
and articulated by persisting formations. No fixed collection of live cells is
the glider — the formation continually reinstantiates through different cells.

A caution on the second exhibit. The cellular automaton is a poor exemplar of
the theory and an excellent exemplar of the error. Its rule is external to it:
the field does not transform itself, it is transformed by something not in it.
So it should carry cross-cutting identity (A3) and nothing else — never the
ontology. Its virtue here is that everything about it is transparent, which is
why the mistake stands out naked in it.

## The cone, as scaffolding

A cone viewed along one axis presents a circle, along another a triangle.
Neither is manufactured by the viewer; the geometry constrains what can appear.
And the triangle is not the circle, though both are of the cone:

    △ ≠ ○     and yet     △ ≡ ○   with respect to the cone

This is useful for one purpose only: showing that two expressions can be
neither identical nor arbitrary, and that their equivalence is grounded by the
one thing both express.

It must then be discarded, and the reason is in the theory's vocabulary rule. A
projection requires a projector. The cone does not present a circle; light
presents a circle, onto a screen, along an axis someone selected, and the
apparatus is not part of the cone. Kept as the picture rather than the ladder,
the metaphor reintroduces the agent the theory excludes.

The wave has no such defect. Nothing selects an axis.

## Emergence

The glider is the standard example of emergence, and by T3 nothing emerges.

Emergence requires the differentiated articulation to be real and the formation
to appear above it once complexity suffices. That is a production relation
between `δ` and `μ`, and T3 shows no such relation obtains — including in the
case, like this one, where `δ` fixes the state at every instant. The glider is
not produced by the cells. It is the same whole individuated the other way, and
it was available throughout.

**But *illusion* is one word too strong, and the overshoot does damage.** An
illusion is something one seems to see that is not there. The glider is there:
A3 holds of it, so the second articulation is genuine. What is illusory is the
*emerging* — the coming-into-being at a level above.

The glider is real. The emergence is not.

## Reduction is the same mistake

The sentence that comes naturally next — *so it is really just cells* —
collapses everything to `δ` alone, which A2 forbids as directly as emergence
does.

**Emergentism and reduction are one mistake with opposite signs.** Both take
levels as given, both take one level as fundamental, and they disagree only
about which. The emergentist says the whole is more than its parts; the
reductionist says it is nothing but. Both sentences presuppose the comparison is
meaningful, and by A1 it is not: the two articulations are not quantities of one
thing, to be greater or less than one another.

Denying the shared premise is not a compromise between the two positions.

## Two things the word conflates

Separating them is most of the work.

**A shift of articulation.** Gliders are formations not named in the rule. This
is the error unmixed. Nothing occurred; the criterion of identity changed.

**A change in time.** At `t=0` a random field holds no gliders; at `t=500` it
holds several. Something did occur — but this is change described wholly within
`μ`, the whole modulating into formations it was not in. It is not a passage
from one level to another.

One word covering both a shift of articulation and a change in time is how the
confusion sustains itself.

In passing: the cellular automaton is the weakest available showcase for strong
emergence, since complete derivability is guaranteed by construction. The
canonical illustration is a case in which the strong thesis is trivially false.

## What the talk was tracking

The intuition is not empty, and T3 names its well-posed replacement.

The genuine question is why one rule is remarkable when most are not. Most
rules afford no second articulation whatever: they die, or they go to noise, and
no persistent formation arises for form-identity to attach to. That difference
is a fact about the rule and not about anyone's attention.

So the intuition that something notable occurs is correct. The description — a
new level appeared — is wrong. What varies between rules is **affordance**:
whether the whole admits a second articulation at all.

This is the general shape of the conversion. A production question, which is
malformed, becomes an affordance question, which is hard and well-posed.

## An objection that survives

Affordance is not static. A random field affords no second articulation, there
being no persistent formation for form-identity to attach to; after settling, it
does. So what articulations are available did genuinely change, and *nothing
emerged* is too quick if it is taken to mean that nothing changed.

What changed is the whole's organization, such that a second criterion of
identity gains purchase. That is still not a level appearing above another; it
is the whole becoming articulable in a way it was not. Should anyone wish to
call that emergence, the word has been redefined to contain no levels — at which
point it is no longer the thesis in dispute.

## A warning about this document

A theory that dissolves everything explains nothing.

*It is only a shift of articulation* is a universal solvent, and applied
carelessly it will deny production relations that genuinely obtain. T3 does not
say there are no production relations. It says none obtains **between two
articulations of one whole** — and whether two candidate descriptions are that
is exactly what T2 decides.

So no case belongs in this family until it has passed T2. A pair of
descriptions whose identity relations coincide is one description twice (A3),
and the dissolution does not apply to it.

## Pending

Other members of this family, not yet written:

- **Vitalism.** How does life arise from chemistry. Settled historically, and
  settled in this shape — no vital principle was found and none was refuted;
  the question stopped being asked. A precedent in which this dissolution
  already ran and was vindicated.
- **The hard problem.** How does experience arise from physical processing. The
  target case, and the one requiring most care: the disanalogy is that the
  second articulation is claimed to be available only from within, which the
  theory answers by denying that either articulation is a viewing at all.
- **Symbol grounding.**
- **Temperature and molecular motion.** Worth testing precisely because it is
  filed as a success of reduction. Same temperature, many microstates, which
  looks like A3. If the supposed triumph is a two-articulation case, that is a
  result; if it fails T2, the test has earned its keep by refusing something.
