# Cohering

## The whole is prior to its distinctions

A whole is not assembled from parts. Its parts are ways it distinguishes
itself. This is the first commitment and everything else depends on it.

The reverse picture — parts, then relations, then an integrated whole — is the
one to refuse. It is not merely a different emphasis. It makes unity an
achievement, something that succeeds or fails at being reached from below. Here
unity is not reached. It is prior, and multiplicity is one of the ways it
articulates itself.

This is why *integration* is the wrong word for anything in this account.
Integration presupposes separates to be integrated.

## Two self-articulations

A whole that changes articulates itself in two ways. Neither is beneath the
other, neither produces the other, and neither is the whole.

**Differentiation** — the whole distinguishes within itself. We resolve
distinguishable elements and follow the relations among them: molecule to
molecule, gear to gear, cell to cell. Identity attaches to the differentiated
participants.

**Modulation** — the whole varies as itself. We do not begin from elements and
ask how they combine; we follow extended formations of the whole through
change: a wave, a vortex, a melody, a living movement. Identity attaches to the
persisting formation.

The water does not assemble molecules into a wave. The water waves.

### A note on the word "modulation"

Two senses are in circulation and only one is meant.

In engineering, modulation is transitive: a carrier is modulated *by* a signal.
That sense carries a hidden agent and is not what is meant here.

In music it is intransitive: a passage modulates to the dominant. Nothing
modulates it. It is the same grammatical shape as *the water waves* — the whole
is the subject of its own varying, and there is no second thing acting on it.

This is a general constraint on the vocabulary of this account, not a quirk of
one word. Every term we have had to discard failed the same test. Projection
requires a projector. Integration requires an integrator. Modulation in the
engineering sense requires a modulator. A term is admissible here only if it can
be said intransitively of the whole.

## Their identities cross-cut

The two articulations do not merely describe differently. They individuate
differently, and their criteria of sameness fail to coincide in both directions.

The same water parcel participates in many waves. The same wave persists through
continuously changing parcels. So sameness under differentiation is not sameness
under modulation, and neither reduces to the other.

This cross-cutting is what makes them two articulations rather than one
articulation under two names. A description that merely relabels another will
covary with it perfectly and individuate identically. That is decoration.

## Yet they covary

Perturb the whole and both articulations change correspondingly. They are not
two pictures that happen to agree. They are one happening, and they move
together because there is nothing else for them to do.

Change the dynamics of the water and the parcel account and the wave account
change together, without either being adjusted to match the other.

## The test

An articulation is non-decorative when:

1. **Its identity criteria cross-cut those of the other.** Sameness under one is
   not sameness under the other, in both directions.
2. **Their transformations covary under perturbation of the whole.** Not
   correspondence arranged between the two descriptions, but correspondence
   forced through the whole they both articulate.

A third clause is tempting — *neither articulation exhausts the whole* — and it
should be resisted, because it is not checkable and because it is already
contained in the first. There is no access to the whole apart from its
articulations, so no independent inspection could confirm it.

The apparent counterexample is instructive. In a cellular automaton the cell
states exhaust the state completely: cells at *t* fix cells at *t+1*, and
nothing is left over. It looks as though differentiation exhausts the whole and
modulation is bookkeeping.

But exhausting the *state* is not exhausting the *whole*, because the whole is a
happening and not a configuration. That the same glider persisted is a fact
about the transformation, and no instantaneous cell-fact contains it. The
vertical carries identity-through-change that the horizontal does not hold even
when complete at every moment.

So the third clause reduces to the first — and the reduction is a result, not a
tidying. It is why the whole has to be understood as a happening.

## The cone, and its limit

A cone viewed along one axis presents a circle; viewed along another, a
triangle. Neither is manufactured by the viewer. The geometry constrains what
can appear. And the triangle is not the circle, though both are genuinely of the
cone:

    △ ≠ ○     and yet     △ ≡ ○   with respect to the cone

This is useful scaffolding. It shows that two expressions can be neither
identical nor arbitrary, and that their equivalence is grounded by the one thing
of which both are constrained expressions.

It must then be discarded, because a projection requires a projector. The cone
does not present a circle; light presents a circle, onto a screen, along an axis
someone selected. The apparatus is not part of the cone. Retained as the picture
rather than the ladder, the metaphor hands back the observer this account exists
to evict.

The wave has no such defect. The crest is not a projection of the water taken by
anyone. Nothing selects an axis. The water waves.

**Self-articulation, not projection, is primitive.**

For the same reason the cellular automaton is the weaker exemplar and should not
carry the ontology. Its rule is external to it: the universe does not transform
itself, it is transformed by something not in it. The glider remains an
excellent illustration of cross-cutting identity — no fixed set of cells is the
glider — and should be used for that and nothing more.

## Two orders, and why the difference matters

Ontologically the whole grounds both articulations:

    whole → (differentiation, modulation)

Epistemically we travel the other way. We encounter two cross-cutting but
covarying articulations and become licensed to treat them as expressions of one
whole:

    (differentiation, modulation) → whole

The orders are inverse, and this is not a defect. It is the ordinary situation
of inferring a solid from its aspects.

But it has a consequence that must be stated, because ignoring it is how this
project went wrong before: **a proposed cohering cannot be checked by inspecting
the whole.** There is nothing to inspect until the test passes. You check
cross-cutting and covariance, and the whole is what you become licensed to posit.

The alternative — declare the whole first, then derive two aspects for it —
feels like rigor and is the failure mode. It produces schemas with slots, and
slots are always satisfiable.

## Rhythm remains open

Something is common to both articulations. In the clock it appears through the
interaction of parts; in the ocean through the movement of the wave; in the
orchestra through the unfolding of the music. The same happening is expressed
differently, and something of its temporal organization persists across the
difference. We have been calling this **rhythm**.

It is not a third articulation. There is no third camera angle. And it is not
adequately defined as an invariant, because many trivial quantities are
invariant across the two.

Its structure is not settled, and this document does not settle it. But the hole
has a definite shape, and three constraints hold of whatever fills it:

- it is not itself an articulation;
- it is what the covariance consists in — the correspondence does not *reveal*
  the rhythm to anyone, it is what the rhythm amounts to;
- it is an organization of change, not of states.

*Departure → relation → return* is a candidate against those constraints. It is
not a definition, and it should not be promoted to one by the precision of its
surroundings. An elegant frame with an empty centre is a recognizable way to be
wrong; the honest response is to mark the centre open rather than to furnish it
with a slogan.

## Summary

The whole is prior to its distinctions.

In changing, it articulates itself in two cross-cutting ways: by differentiating
within itself, and by modulating as itself.

The two have different criteria of identity yet covary, because they are the
same happening.

Their common organization of change is what we have been calling rhythm, and its
structure is not yet settled.
